# Script rules from your own retention

The loop:
1. **Friday:** `python3 research/retention/retention.py` pulls every published Short's YouTube retention curve and Instagram watch time and writes `REPORT.md` (drops lined up with the words and the layout on screen).
2. Read REPORT.md against the rules below. A rule the new Reels keep breaking and still holding up = **keep**. A rule the numbers contradict = **retire** it here with the date. A new pattern seen in 3+ Reels = **add** it as `testing`.
3. avatar-shorts step 2 checks every new script against this file before it goes to you for review. The table at the bottom tracks what each new Reel tested and what happened.

Numbers start small, so a rule needs the same pattern in several Reels, not one hit. Fill the Evidence column with your own REPORT.md numbers as they come in.

## Where the starting rules came from

The rules below are a starting set, taken from the retention curves of a first batch of about 15 Reels in this kit's format. Your own REPORT.md decides which stay. What those curves showed:

- **About half the viewers swipe before the first second counts.** The openers that kept the most were a big contrast number and money.
- **Of those who stay, most leave between 4 and 10 seconds.** On Instagram the average watch is often under 15 s, so this window is all Instagram ever sees.
- **The second sentence decides it.** Reels that said some version of *"It's called X. It plugs into Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, and it does three things"* or *"Here's how it works, in three steps"* at 4-7 s were half gone by 8 s. The ones that held used that slot for the viewer instead: their objection and your own before/after, their pain, or an open loop that only the end closes.
- **Face beats lose people faster** than split and full-graphics beats, and in this format the first face beat tends to land right in the 4-10 s drop zone.
- **Cuts cost:** viewers leave faster within half a second of a layout change. The worst openers flip split → full → split → full every 2-3 s.
- **The Reel people watched to the end and replayed** explained one idea in plain words on full-screen graphics from about 3.5 s and never flipped back.
- **Long-form:** an episode that pitched the free file in its first 30 seconds lost most of its viewers by 0:30; episodes that went straight into building kept most of theirs. Don't sell the free file in the first minute.

## The rules

| # | Rule | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hook is one breath with a contrast number, money or a result on screen at frame 0. | keep | |
| 2 | **Seconds 4-10 are about the viewer, never the tool's name.** Say their objection or pain, then your before/after, or open a loop that only the end closes. | keep | |
| 3 | **No name-first sentences.** Never "It's called X" or "Number one, X." The name rides inside a benefit: "Ponytail cuts almost half your tokens". | keep | |
| 4 | No signposts: no "it does three things", "here's how it works", "it plugs into Claude Code, Codex or Cursor". The numbers go on screen (the tally), not in the voice. | keep | |
| 5 | **No face beat before 10 s.** Keep the payoff on screen through 4-10 s; your face comes back for the receipt and the CTA. | testing | |
| 6 | **At most one layout change in the first 10 s**, and it moves (whip, push), never a hard flip. | testing | |
| 7 | Plant the open loop in the hook ("…and the last one checks the other four") so the end pays it off. | testing | |
| 8 | Shorter wins until the middle holds: aim 35-40 s. | testing | |
| 9 | Long-form: one promise line, then straight into building. The free file is pitched after the first result, not in the intro. | keep | |
