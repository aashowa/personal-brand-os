# YouTube growth playbook: 0 → 1K → 10K → 100K

The rules, by stage. Distilled from a full-time creator's growth talk plus common practice; for titles and thumbnails in detail, `research/packaging/PLAYBOOK.md` wins where they overlap.

## Principles

1. **Attention is earned.** Every video answers: why would a stranger care? The "selfish video" (only interesting to you) is the most common beginner mistake.

   | Level | Example | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | Selfish | "My AI setup" | No reason for a stranger to watch |
   | Generic | "How to use AI in your business" | Clear, but everyone has made it |
   | Stranger-value | "I handed my worst weekly task to AI for 30 days" | Specific, a clear payoff, curiosity |

2. **Pick a lane before you post.** Lane = audience (who, specifically) + value (what changes for them). It is a hypothesis: commit, test, adjust. Random posting builds a channel YouTube can't recommend.
3. **Quantity builds quality early.** At one video a week, 100 videos take two years; at two a week, one. In 0→1K, reps beat polish: the gap between your taste and your skill closes only through reps.
4. **Packaging is the biggest lever from 1K to 10K.** Title and thumbnail are the front door. Good packaging works on curiosity (a loop to close), desire (something they want) or fear (something they want to avoid).
5. **Outliers become recipes.** An outlier is a video at 4x+ your channel average. A recipe = audience + value + format + vibe, pulled from what already worked and kept the same across a series.
6. **Raise the view floor from 10K to 100K.** View floor = the daily views you get without uploading. Three levers: more bangers, fewer flops, higher quality without lower quantity.
7. **At scale, the idea matters most.** Breakout signals: a version has worked big before, the idea appeals well beyond your niche, and it clearly triggers curiosity, desire or fear.

## 0 → 1K

**Before you post**
1. Define the lane: what you could talk about for years, what you have actually done, which creator you admire and why people watch them.
2. Launch with four videos at once, each standing alone and pointing to the next. Together they say what the channel is and why to stay, and they teach the algorithm who your viewer is.
3. Map the binge loop: end each video with a spoken pointer to a related video and put it on the end screen. Viewers watching several of your videos in a row is a strong signal.

**Then post consistently:** at least once a week, ideally twice. Each upload better or different from the last.

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lane switching | Pivoting after two weak videos | Give a lane at least 10 focused videos before judging it |
| Never changing | 50 similar videos, all flat | The hypothesis was wrong; make a new one |
| Platform sprawl | YouTube, TikTok and Instagram at once from zero | Build momentum on one first |
| Early expectations | "Three months and nothing worked" | Plan on one to two years |

**Find your signal:** after 10+ videos, look for the one at 2 to 4x your normal views, read its comments, note the question or format that keeps coming up. That is your first recipe. Do more of it.

## Content rules

**Do**
- **Stranger test:** would someone with no idea who you are still want to watch?
- **Cohesion:** four videos watched back to back should make it obvious who the channel is for.
- **80% rule:** would 80% of the people who liked your last video want this one? If not, it's the wrong video.
- **Brainstorm in volume:** 30 title ideas per video, in four five-minute rounds: your own head; title structures from five channels with similar value and a different audience; phrasing from outliers on your topic; then an AI pass for variations. Narrow to 3 to 5, pick one.
- **Study what worked,** in your niche and next to it. In thumbnails, find the main character (what the eye hits first) and the supporting characters (context).

**Don't**
- Selfish videos.
- Random topic switching.
- Brand deals before 10K unless you need the money: they slow production and shift focus to fulfilment.
- Creative paralysis after early success: keep shipping.
- Clones: a direct copy of a hit never beats the original. Ask how to make it yours.

**Titles:** curiosity gaps, explicit desire or fear, specific enough to be believed (numbers, time frames, outcomes), and the video must deliver the promise or retention drops.

**Thumbnails:** design for a feeling within two seconds, not for looks. Start from a proven composition, then make it yours. Pitch each concept in one sentence: "We see X with Y, which makes you feel Z." Deliver what the thumbnail shows: if it shows a dashboard, the video opens on that dashboard.

**Hooks (first 30 seconds):** answer "why keep watching this video, now?" before the viewer asks it. Show the outcome first, then the setup, then the process.

## How recommendations work

YouTube learns who likes you from who watches, what else they watch, and how many of your videos they watch in one session. Chains of your videos are the strongest signal, which is why the four-video launch and the binge loop work.

| Metric | Tells YouTube | Improve with |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate | Is the packaging compelling? | Titles and thumbnails |
| Average view duration | Does the video deliver the promise? | Stronger hooks, tighter editing |
| Session watch time | Do they want more from you? | End-screen pointers, a cohesive channel |
| View floor | Channel health and discoverability | More bangers, fewer flops |

## Diagnosing a flop

| Problem | Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Click | Low CTR | Rework the title, the thumbnail or both. Old videos can be revived this way. |
| Watch | Retention drops early | Open the retention graph. First 30 seconds = hook. Mid-video = pacing or value. Steady slide = the content doesn't match the promise. |
| Idea | CTR and retention fine, views still flat | The audience was too small or the idea didn't reach beyond existing fans. Add a filter to how you pick ideas. |

## Banger filters

Run every idea through:
- Feasible with the time and tools you have?
- Would a stranger want it? Would 80% of your audience?
- Can it hit your view goal? Is there prior proof?
- Does it fit a recipe, or start one?
- Can you make a strong title and a strong thumbnail for it?
- Can you make it uniquely yours?

## Distribution

- Long-form on YouTube first; everything else feeds from it. The email list is second (an owned audience). Short-form clips help discovery but must not eat long-form production time.
- Don't promote a video until its packaging is locked.
- A video 2x+ above expectations in its first 48 hours: repurpose it now, and write down why it worked so the recipe can repeat.
- A question asked five or more times in the comments is the next video.

## Quick reference

| Stage | What matters most | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| 0 → 1K | Lane, consistency, reps | Giving up early, random posting |
| 1K → 10K | Titles and thumbnails | Treating packaging as an extra |
| 10K → 100K | Idea quality, a unique angle | Copying without making it yours |

Every video: passes the stranger test · 30+ title candidates before choosing · a thumbnail that triggers a feeling · an end-screen pointer to the next video · fits the recipe or starts one on purpose.

Every flop: click, watch or idea problem? Fix that one.
