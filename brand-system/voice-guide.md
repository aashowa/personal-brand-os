## Writing rules

### Do

- Open with the operational fact, number, friction or observation, not a broad promise.
- Write in first person and let the process unfold: what I saw → what I tried → what changed → what that does and does not prove.
- Use your own spoken connectors where they earn their place: {{your connectors}}.
- Keep claims grounded. State uncertainty, a human check or a limitation when the evidence needs it.
- Address the audience as peers. {{How you address them out loud, and when that fits in writing.}}
- End with a low-pressure next step: a question, an invitation to ask for the breakdown, or one action.

### Do not

- Turn your voice into agency copy: no "unlock", "revolutionary", "seamlessly", "leverage" or generic thought-leadership language.
- Replace real speech with framework labels ("three key pillars") unless you actually talk that way.
- Manufacture certainty, results, scale or a client lesson. If it is not cleared proof, frame it as an observation or leave it out.
- Perform informality with fake "um"s, grammar mistakes, slang or exaggerated excitement. The voice is conversational because it is specific and candid, not because it is messy.

## Default story shape

Use this shape for a Reel, a long-form segment, a LinkedIn post, a caption or a script unless the format needs something tighter:

1. **Receipt:** what happened? Start with the number, mismatch or moment that earned attention.
2. **Context:** why did it matter to your business?
3. **Walkthrough:** what did you actually look at, change or test?
4. **Honest boundary:** what can't you prove, what still needed a human check, what wouldn't you do?
5. **Takeaway:** what should the viewer look at or try next?
6. **Invitation:** offer the exact breakdown if there is a useful follow-up, not a forced CTA.

## Quality check before approval

- Could you say this aloud without sounding like you're reading a brand deck?
- Does the first line contain a real receipt, a real problem or a clear observation?
- Does the copy explain what happened before explaining the tool?
- Are the claim, scope and certainty truthful to the approved evidence?
- Did enough human judgment stay in to make the lesson credible?
- Does the close invite a useful next conversation instead of pushing a generic conversion?

## How to use this guide

- **Scripts:** write for speech first. Keep sentence lengths and connectors natural, then remove only what makes recording harder.
- **LinkedIn and captions:** keep the same proof-and-walkthrough structure, smoothed only for readability. Not corporate prose.
- **Titles and thumbnails:** lead with the operational tension or receipt, never a vague promise.
- **AI editing and repurposing:** keep qualified claims and natural turns of phrase. Any generated copy still needs your approval before it goes out.

## Calibration policy

Version 1 can come from one sample. Add recordings from different contexts (casual explanation, disagreement, story, short-form delivery) before changing the core rules. New samples refine the guide; they never replace your actual words with a generic "creator voice".
