# How you talk

**How to build this file**

1. Collect your own speech: transcripts of your long-form videos, calls or recordings where you explain your work. Aim for 15,000 to 20,000 words across at least four recordings. Only your own voice counts: an avatar or a voice clone reading a script is not your speech.
2. Have your agent read every transcript end to end and fill the numbers table below (method in each row).
3. For each "How you..." section, pick three to five snippets: your exact words, grammar included, each cited `[EP03 4:12]`. Fix brand names the transcription misheard in [brackets]. The snippets are there to hear your rhythm, not to paste as lines.
4. Fill the signature phrases and "never does" lists from the counts, not from memory.
5. After each new episode, add its transcript to Sources, update the counts and swap in better snippets (keep the citations). Don't drop older episodes: the voice should come from all of them.

## Writing for the teleprompter

1. Short lines, one idea each, in your word order. When a line is too written, you'll stumble on it; note which lines took several takes and why.
2. Open the way you open. No greeting unless that's genuinely you.
3. Put a real number in the first 15 seconds, one of your own receipts.
4. One humble line.
5. Your audience word once or twice. Your usual connectors to start beats. At most one of each filler-ish word.
6. End on the whole idea in one line, then your sign-off.
7. Two habits scripts don't copy: jargon you say day to day but a beginner wouldn't (say the beginner word, for example "the Claude app", never "terminal"), and your grammar slips. Write it the way you'd say it on a good take.
